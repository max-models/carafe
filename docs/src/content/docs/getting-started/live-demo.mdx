The relation is Boeuf's local gradient-drift relation (his Eq. (1),
[arXiv:2211.17051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.17051)) for the ionisation region of a DC magnetron:
magnetised electrons with collisions and finite Larmor radius, drifting by E×B, diamagnetic and
grad-B drifts, unmagnetised cold ions and Poisson's equation. The sliders start at his point,
1.8 cm from the target, where the fastest mode grows at about 10<sup>7</sup> s<sup>-1</sup> at a
wavelength of 1.6 cm and moves at about 15 km/s, far slower than the E×B drift. The density gradient
drives the mode and the grad-B drift of the falling field works against it: with the grad-B drift
on, it grows fast only while |<i>L</i><sub>n</sub>| is below about |<i>L</i><sub>B</sub>|/2. Try
<i>L</i><sub>B</sub> = −3 mm or <i>L</i><sub>n</sub> = −5 mm.

The same scan as a script:

```python
import numpy as np

from wellbalanced.driftfluid2d import boeuf_dispersion_roots

k = np.linspace(20.0, 3000.0, 150)  # 1/m
roots = [boeuf_dispersion_roots(kk, 0.8e15, -2200.0, 6e-3, 11.4, -2.2e-3, -6e-3, 2e7) for kk in k]
growth = np.array([r.imag.max() for r in roots])  # 1/s
print(f"{growth.max():.3g} /s at a wavelength of {2 * np.pi / k[growth.argmax()]:.3g} m")
```

The full local theory, with kinetic ions and the compressibility of the drifts, is
`wellbalanced.dispersion`; the nonlocal eigenmodes across a profile are in
`wellbalanced.dispersion.eigenmodes`.
